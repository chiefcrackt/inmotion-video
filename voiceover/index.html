Voiceover

Turn a script into a voiceover with Kokoro, a free, open text-to-speech model, on your computer. You get a WAV for CapCut plus word timing that Caption Maker reads.

Computer only — local launcher required

This tool does not work on phones or tablets yet. Open it on the Windows computer running the Voiceover launcher, then AirDrop or sync the WAV to your phone.

Computer only

Open your local studio

  1. On your Windows computer, open the repo’s local-voiceover folder and double-click Start Voiceover.cmd. The first start installs the engine (a few minutes), and the first voiceover downloads the voice model (about 350 MB).
  2. It runs in the background, with no window to keep open. Open the studio below; its Stop button at the bottom ends it.
  3. Paste a script, pick a voice or blend, press Make voiceover, and download the WAV, word timing and captions.

What it does

Voices and blends
28 English voices (American and British), each with a preview. Blend two or three into a voice of your own; T.im narrator (60% Puck, 40% Michael) is the default.
Speakers
Start a line with a name and a colon, like Coach:, and give each speaker their own voice. A [pause 1.5] line adds silence.
Pronunciation
A saved list of words and how to say them, such as T.im as “Tim”. Captions still show the word as written.
Lines
Each line can be played and given its own speed or pause. Making it again only speaks the lines that changed.
Downloads
A 48 kHz WAV at −16 LUFS, Word JSON for Caption Maker (words light up as they are spoken), and SRT and VTT captions.

Kokoro’s model and voices are Apache-2.0 licensed, so voiceovers can be used commercially. Scripts stay on your computer; after the first download it works offline.
