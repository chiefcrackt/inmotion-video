Which look?

Dark design Start here

The vinyl covers the design and you bleach around it. The design stays the shirt color inside an acid-wash spray.

  • The splatter is the background, so it pairs with the acid wash look.
  • Forgiving: bleach creeping under an edge only nibbles the letters.
  • Weed away everything except the design.

Light design

The design is cut out of a sheet of vinyl and bleach only reaches the letters, so they come out lighter than the shirt.

  • Cleanest on a solid dark shirt; gets lost on a busy acid wash.
  • Cover everything around the sheet too (paper or plastic).
  • Weed out the design and keep the surrounding sheet.

Same cut file either way. Only the weeding changes.

What to get
  • 100% cotton in black, navy or charcoal. Polyester and most blends barely change. Black usually bleaches to rust or orange; navy to pink or tan.
  • Permanent adhesive vinyl (like Oracal 651): it grips fabric better than removable vinyl. Plus transfer tape.
  • Regular bleach, a fine-mist spray bottle, and gel bleach (like Clorox gel) for crisp brushed edges.
  • Cardboard to slip inside the shirt, gloves, eye protection.
  • Hydrogen peroxide or a bleach neutralizer (Bleach Stop / anti-chlor).
How to make one
  1. Prewash and dry the shirt with no fabric softener.
  2. Upload the SVG in Design Space and check the size before cutting. Cut on the vinyl setting, not mirrored.
  3. Weed for the look you picked and lift it with transfer tape. The dots and periods come along on the tape.
  4. Cardboard inside the shirt. Smooth the vinyl on and burnish every edge hard. Most bleed starts at a lifted edge.
  5. Outdoors or with good airflow, mist bleach (about 1 part bleach to 1 part water) from about a foot away, and flick drops off a brush for splatter. Go light: it keeps darkening for a few minutes.
  6. When the color looks right (usually 5–15 minutes), peel the vinyl and rinse in cold water right away.
  7. Soak in water with a splash of hydrogen peroxide, or use the neutralizer as directed. Then wash the shirt on its own.

Never mix bleach with vinegar, ammonia or other cleaners. Test on an old shirt first: every shirt bleaches to a different color.

Line thickness

Bleach wicks a little under the vinyl, so very thin lines blur or fill in. Each file lists its thinnest line. Around 0.16" or more is comfortable; below about 0.12", make it bigger or brush gel bleach on instead of spraying.
